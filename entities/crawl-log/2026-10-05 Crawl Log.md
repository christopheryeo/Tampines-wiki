#### Lessons learned

- **A Singapore-nexus hit can arrive through purely bilateral diplomatic protocol, not only crisis or
  enforcement news.** Today's single genuine nexus hit was a routine-seeming bilateral courtesy
  meeting (Qatar's Deputy PM calling on Singapore's visiting Defence Minister) — a reminder that the
  nexus bar should keep triaging every direct Singapore/SAF/MINDEF mention regardless of how
  low-drama the framing looks, rather than implicitly weighting toward conflict/enforcement framings
  as the last two runs' genuine hits (a DOJ indictment, an export-control case) might suggest.
- **Acronym and name collisions keep dominating the Singapore-nexus scan's false-positive rate.**
  11 of 12 hits today were false positives from two clusters: "SAF" matching Sudanese Armed Forces
  (not Singapore's), and "Singapore" matching an Indian state chief minister's business-infrastructure
  study trip. Both collision classes have now recurred across multiple daily runs (Texas school
  district "ISD" and a Nvidia-grocery-REIT "acquire" collision were noted on 2026-10-04) — the scan
  remains a human-reviewed shortlist tool, not an auto-accept filter, by design, and continues to earn
  its keep at that role.
- **A story's absence from its own dedicated topic does not mean the story is unrepresented.** Four of
  today's seven accepted articles (Okinawa, Mecca Pact, Shoebridge/Australia, Jaishankar-Paparo) were
  discovered through a *different* topic's pass-1/pass-2 query than their eventual canonical topic
  assignment, because keyword overlap routed them into an adjacent topic's narrow (≤500) result set
  while their "home" topic query either also returned them or was itself high-volume and untriaged.
  This is a structural argument for keeping the per-topic query net wide rather than narrowing queries
  further for precision.
