#### Lessons learned

- **A keyword-only Singapore-nexus scan can miss a genuine direct hit when the institutional
  marker is in the body, not the headline.** The Bersama Lima FPDA exercise article's title names
  only the UK ships and the exercise; "Singapore" appears only in the body as one of five
  participating nations. Topics with an inherent Singapore/regional institutional hook (FPDA,
  bilateral-defence-relations, asean-defence-cooperation) warrant a body read on their own in-window
  candidates even when the title-only keyword scan is clean, rather than relying on the scan alone.
- **"Urges"/"plans to"/anonymous-sourced "contingency" framing is a useful signal for dropping a
  candidate under the strict daily bar.** Both softer candidates dropped this run (the Senate letter
  urging Taiwan arms deliveries, the anonymously-sourced Kyiv embassy evacuation-planning report)
  were requests or unconfirmed internal deliberation, not dated state-level decisions or incidents —
  a useful discriminator alongside the existing opinion/analysis/retrospective exclusions.
- **Deriving pass-2 keyword variants mechanically from `canonical-topics.yaml`'s regex `keywords`
  field works but trades precision for recall.** Stripping `\b`/`\s+`/alternation-group regex syntax
  into plain phrases correctly surfaced real in-window coverage for several previously-zero topics
  (e.g. `asean-defence-cooperation`, `taiwan-strait`, `hybrid-cognitive-warfare`), but single-word
  broad terms (`research`, `defense`, `training`) also produced very large, mostly off-topic result
  sets (the 26 high-volume topics), consistent with the plan's existing high-volume-recall caution.
