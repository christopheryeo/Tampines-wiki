#### Lessons learned

- **A regex-based shortlist tuned on one day's vocabulary will miss a different day's top-tier
  candidate — a second, broader pass caught one the first missed.** The base shortlist (war/strike/
  escalat/casualty terms in title) missed the Poland Patriot/civil-defence-budget story and the Taiwan
  NMESIS story because neither title contains a war/attack keyword. A supplementary regex pass adding
  deploy/force-posture/civil-defence-budget terms surfaced both, netting one genuine accept (Poland)
  and confirming one near-miss duplicate (Taiwan). The shortlist is explicitly documented as "a triage
  aid, not an auto-accept filter," but this is the first day with concrete evidence that a single fixed
  regex under-covers major-but-non-kinetic developments (budget announcements, force-posture moves,
  deployments) as reliably as it covers kinetic ones (strikes, attacks). Worth folding deploy/force-
  posture/budget terms into the standing shortlist regex rather than re-deriving them ad hoc each day.
- **A same-event duplicate can arrive under a materially different canonical URL days apart, defeating
  the hash-based gate; only a content-level check catches it.** The Taiwan NMESIS/Yonaguni deployment
  was first reported by Taipei Times on 2026-10-03 (as a forthcoming plan) and resurfaced via
  antiwar.com on 2026-10-06/07 (reporting it as now-disclosed, with added Chinese-expert reaction).
  Different outlet, different URL, different SHA-256 articleId — the mechanical duplicate gate would
  have cascaded it as new. Only a targeted content grep (searching for the distinguishing proper noun,
  "Yonaguni") surfaced the 2026-10-03 note. This mirrors the 2026-10-06 lesson about a long-running war
  generating incidental hits, but is a distinct failure mode: here it is the *same specific development*
  resurfacing through reaction/follow-up coverage, not merely the same broader conflict. A reusable
  mitigation: for every "strong candidate" surfaced by the shortlist, run one targeted grep on its most
  distinctive proper noun (a place name, system name, or named official) against `entities/article/**`
  before normalizing, rather than relying solely on the canonical-URL/articleId hash gate.
