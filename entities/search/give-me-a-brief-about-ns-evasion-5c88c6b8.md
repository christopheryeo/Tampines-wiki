The topic covers two separate Enlistment Act prosecutions. The larger story is [[amos-yee|Amos Yee]] (27): deported from the US and arrested by CMPB enlistment inspectors at Changi Airport on 20 Mar 2026, then charged the same day under the Enlistment Act — failing to report for pre-enlistment medical screening from Apr 2016 to Mar 2026, and leaving Singapore without a valid exit permit across two periods (Dec 2015-Apr 2016 and Dec 2016-Mar 2026). MINDEF and MHA issued a joint statement; the charging drew coverage from roughly 49 outlets. Background recapped across the coverage: Yee left for the US in 2016 following earlier convictions over remarks on religion, was granted US asylum in 2017, was later convicted there of child sex offences (sentenced Dec 2021), paroled and then re-detained for a parole violation, and was picked up by US ICE in Nov 2025 before deportation. On 26 Mar he made his first court reappearance by videolink — CMPB's preliminary investigations were complete, and he was offered S$10,000 bail on three charges, barred from public statements on the case, with a pre-trial conference set for 23 Apr 2026. He was released the same day, collected by his mother, and told media he looked forward to continuing activism and reconnecting with his father, describing US prisons as comparatively comfortable. By 28 Mar an email attributed to him was circulating online outlining plans for a Gandhi-inspired movement on free speech, housing and healthcare.

The second, unrelated case is [[edmond-yao|Edmond Yao Zhi Hai]] (47), a dual citizen convicted on 23 Mar 2026 of defaulting on NS enlistment dating back to 1997. The court rejected his defence that Indonesian citizenship exempted him — the judge ruled it a strict-liability offence and that he could not "cherry-pick" which laws to follow. Sentencing was adjourned to April, with exposure of up to three years' jail and/or a S$5,000 fine.
