## Question
Tell me more about Donald Trump

## Answer
Donald Trump has been a central figure in the escalating Iran war and the broader geopolitical maneuvering around it this March. A week into the conflict, he sharply raised the stakes by demanding Iran's "unconditional surrender" and insisting the US should have a say in choosing Iran's next leader, after Ayatollah Khamenei was killed in the war's opening strikes — a move that rattled European and Wall Street markets even as Iran's president said mediation efforts were underway.

He's also been feuding publicly with UK PM Keir Starmer, dismissing Britain's offer to send aircraft carriers to the Middle East ("we don't need them any longer") and saying he'd "remember" the UK's initial reluctance to let the US use its bases to strike Iran — while also sniping at Starmer over unrelated issues like his interest in buying Greenland.

Separately, he's been managing the US-China relationship: he has a landmark visit to Beijing planned for 31 March–2 April, the first sitting US president to visit China since 2017, with tariffs and rare earths on the agenda — though it's since been narrowed to a Beijing-only itinerary due to scheduling and security concerns. China's foreign minister has been framing recent Trump-Xi interactions as having stabilised the relationship.

On the Iran conflict itself, Vladimir Putin called him directly with proposals for a quick diplomatic end to the war, describing it as a "very substantial" conversation; the two also touched on Ukraine, with Trump reportedly suggesting Russia's battlefield gains should push Kyiv toward a negotiated ceasefire.
